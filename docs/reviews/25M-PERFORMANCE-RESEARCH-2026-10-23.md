# Research scan: improving the 25M model

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-23. This is a literature-to-experiment note, not evidence that any cited method improves SLM-Tavenel. Results in the cited papers use larger models, different data, and different evaluation protocols unless stated otherwise.

## Most useful findings

| Paper | Finding relevant to this project | What it supports here | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnusson et al. (2025), [DataDecide](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11393) | Across 25 data recipes and 3 seeds, small-scale comparisons can help select pretraining data. Continuous likelihood proxies predicted several downstream rankings better than small-run benchmark accuracy. | Prefer inexpensive, fixed held-out likelihood comparisons to repeatedly selecting recipes by noisy multiple-choice scores. Compare data arms at the same model size and token budget, and replicate promising recipes across seeds. | Their size range reaches 4M parameters but their main predictive target is 1B; their correlations do not guarantee ranking quality for our 25M model or corpus.
| Mizrahi et al. (2025), [BETR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12466) | Benchmark-targeted ranking selects pretraining text similar to benchmark **training examples**; the paper reports gains over DCLM baselines across its studied scales. It also reports benefits with targets disjoint from evaluation. | Could motivate a separately labeled, task-targeted 25M model after establishing disjoint development and test sets. The existing benchmark test items must never be used to select training text. | This would produce a benchmark-targeted model, not evidence of general language ability. The paper's compute and data setting is much larger than ours.
| Marion et al. (2023; revised 2026), [When Less is More](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04564) | In their experiments, middle-perplexity subsets often beat easy/top or low-perplexity selections; one reported result retained the middle 30–50% and slightly beat the full-data baseline. | A candidate pilot is to score a fixed, reviewed corpus with a reference LM, then compare a middle-perplexity subset with a random subset at identical token exposure. | Their strongest scorer was 52B, far larger than SLM-Tavenel 25M. A 25M self-scorer is an unvalidated transfer. This would test the FSC science domain only.
| Marek et al. (2025), [Small Batch Size Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07101) | They find small batches can be competitive when Adam's second-moment decay is adjusted to preserve its half-life in tokens, rather than reusing the same β₂ per step. | If changing the current effective batch of 32,768 token positions/update, scale β₂ by token exposure and compare at matched total tokens. | More optimizer updates and different gradient noise may be slower or behave differently on this 25M architecture and single RTX 4060.
| Schlotthauer et al. (2025), [Pre-Training LLMs on a budget](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08472) | Their AdamW/Lion/Sophia comparison found optimizer rankings depend on the outcome: Sophia had the lowest train/validation loss, while AdamW led downstream evaluation. | Keep downstream metrics separate from language-model loss; do not choose a method solely on training loss. This is directly relevant to interpreting the ongoing Muon transfer trial. | Not a Muon study and not a direct 25M replication.
| Mehta et al. (2025), [Muon: Training and Trade-offs with Latent Attention and MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24406) | This arXiv preprint reports Muon comparisons on 30M–200M decoders and emphasizes large-batch data efficiency, implementation choices, and stability. | It makes Muon worth revisiting at a comparable scale, but only after the failed SLM-Tavenel run's CUDA stability issue is isolated. | The smallest studied scale is above 25M; the paper combines several architecture and optimizer variables, is a preprint, and does not validate this repository's implementation or settings.
| Wang et al. (2026), [Can Small Training Runs Reliably Guide Data Curation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24503) | Their study finds that recipe rankings can flip under small hyperparameter changes; reduced-LR proxy runs better tracked tuned larger-run rankings in their setup. | EXP-0016's same-LR comparison is a controlled comparison of the specified recipe, but does not establish each recipe's best achievable result. A useful follow-up is a predeclared LR sensitivity arm for FSC and wiki mixture, rather than treating the single LR as optimal. | Their evidence spans 23 recipes and much larger training pipelines; transfer to this 25M continuation and its short budget remains unverified.
| Xu et al. (2026), [Data-Constrained Language Model Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06888) | Masked-input regularization (MIR), an auxiliary next-token loss on randomly masked inputs, improves held-out loss over a strongly regularized autoregressive baseline in their 72M–1.4B experiments; headline comparisons use five seeds. | Motivates a small matched local pilot with the same unique-token exposure and a reserved tokenizer mask ID. | The smallest paper model is 72M, well above this 25M model. MIR adds a forward pass, so equal tokens do not mean equal FLOPs or wall time. The result is an extrapolation to test, not a promised gain.
| Wen et al. (2026), [Hyperball Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16899) | The preprint reports fixed Frobenius-norm wrappers for Adam/Muon and token-equivalent gains on Qwen3-style models up to 1.2B parameters. | A possible future optimizer study after the local Muon/CUDA failure is diagnosed; it motivates inspecting matrix norms and update scales before attempting a full run. | Scale, model, and training budget differ greatly; not evidence that Hyperball improves this model. Its optimizer implementation and stability need independent review.
| Zhao et al. (2026), [Rethinking Data Curation in LLM Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05227) | ADAPT proposes online sample reweighting guided by quality signals instead of static filtering or mixing; the paper reports large-scale pretraining and instruction-tuning results. | Could motivate a small, deterministic online-weighting pilot on reviewed data, with fixed token exposure and separate FSC/wiki likelihood outcomes. | The method adds a scoring/reweighting loop and its reported compute and scales are far beyond this local 25M setup. No direct gain should be assumed.
| Khona et al. (2026), [SOAP, Muon, and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20548) | The study examines update-RMS-matched optimizer comparisons at billion-parameter and trillion-token scale and discusses stability of higher-order optimizers. | Reinforces that fair optimizer comparisons need update-scale matching and explicit stability instrumentation; this can guide the design of a later small diagnostic. | The scale gap is enormous, and its systems techniques are not practical evidence for this single-GPU 25M project.

## Experimental priorities

1. **Complete: compare recipe-specific learning rates.** The preregistered EXP-0018/0019 grid added 1.5e-4 cells for FSC-only and 10% wiki to the existing 3e-4 cells. At this fixed budget, the 3e-4 cells had lower BPB on all four held-out sets for both recipes. The lower rate modestly reduced the FSC cost of the wiki mixture but also reduced its wiki-domain gain. This is one seed and only two LR values; a promising recipe still needs a fresh-seed confirmation before a stronger claim. See the [grid report](../../experiments/EXP-0018-0019-25m-learning-rate-grid-2026-10-23.md).
2. **Complete: masked-input regularization pilot.** EXP-0020/0021 matched training and evaluation are complete. MIR raised held-out BPB on all four sets versus its same-seed control, with paired document-bootstrap intervals excluding zero. The one-seed 25M result does not refute the paper, whose experiments start at 72M; it does not support spending more compute on this MIR recipe without a new rationale. The paper-scale transfer gap and extra forward-pass compute remain explicit limits. The next run replicates the earlier 10% wiki/FSC trade-off on a second seed. See the [protocol](../../experiments/EXP-0020-0021-25m-mir-pilot-2026-10-23.md).
3. **Revisit Muon only after an isolated stability diagnosis.** EXP-0015 stopped before its first checkpoint at step 300 with a CUDA illegal-memory-access error. Do not resume its partial run or reuse its output directory. First use a fresh directory and a short, finite-checked run in a quiet GPU window; then preregister a matched comparison if the issue is resolved. New optimizer preprints are motivation, not evidence about our 25M implementation.
4. **Consider perplexity-based pruning only as a clearly scoped FSC-domain pilot.** Compare random and middle-perplexity subsets; keep token exposure constant. First record source revisions, document hashes, and privacy/license status. Keep candidate weights local until those reviews are resolved.
5. **Do not apply BETR to the existing test files.** A later targeted-model study would need licensed benchmark training splits or other development examples, document deduplication against the evaluation split, a predeclared target, and a held-out suite untouched during data selection.
6. **Treat smaller-batch training as a later efficiency ablation.** Recalculate β₂ using the token-based half-life rule, then compare loss per token and task scores at equal token/compute budgets. It is lower priority than recipe tuning while the GPU is shared and slow.

## Latest local evidence

EXP-0016 is complete. At 20% wiki, held-out wiki BPB improved by 0.178 EN / 0.137 FR, while FSC BPB regressed by 0.0035 EN / 0.0025 FR. All six post-hoc multiple-choice intervals include zero. This is a domain trade-off for one matched checkpoint pair, not a general performance win. The same-LR design does not compare data recipes after recipe-specific tuning, as highlighted by Wang et al. (2026). See [EXP-0016](../../experiments/EXP-0016-25m-wiki-mixture-2026-10-23.md).

EXP-0017 is complete. Its 10% and the 20% EXP-0016 treatment use nested deterministic subsets from the same decontaminated candidate stream, so they are related dose points, not independent replications. Against the FSC-only control, 10% wiki lowers held-out wiki BPB by 0.138 EN / 0.104 FR and raises FSC BPB by 0.00136 EN / 0.00093 FR. Compared with the 20% arm, it retains about 76–78% of the wiki BPB reduction and cuts the FSC regression by about 60–64%. All six post-hoc multiple-choice intervals include zero. This is a more balanced observed dose point under one fixed recipe, not a general quality win or a selected optimum. See [EXP-0017](../../experiments/EXP-0017-25m-wiki-10pct-2026-10-23.md) and its [dose-response graph](../exp0017-wiki-dose-response.svg).

The reduced-rate follow-up is complete in the [EXP-0018/0019 grid](../../experiments/EXP-0018-0019-25m-learning-rate-grid-2026-10-23.md). Lowering LR from 3e-4 to 1.5e-4 raised BPB in all four sets for both recipes. Paired document-bootstrap intervals exclude zero, but are conditional on the four checkpoints and do not estimate seed variance. The recipe-by-LR interaction reduces the FSC cost of the wiki mix slightly while weakening its wiki BPB gain. This two-rate pilot is not an exhaustive tuning search or a general performance result.

The completed [EXP-0020/0021](../../experiments/EXP-0020-0021-25m-mir-pilot-2026-10-23.md) MIR pilot found higher BPB on all four held-out sets; the training/evaluation pair used one seed, and the document bootstrap does not estimate seed variance. The cited paper reports results from 72M parameters upward, outside SLM-Tavenel's 25M scale. MIR also increased measured training time by about 1.84? here. The next useful experiment is an independent-seed replication of the previously promising 10% wiki mixture, using the already trained matched control from seed 20261024.

## Current data and release boundary

The current 25M lineage uses the FSC pilot, whose source-rights and privacy review is unresolved. These candidate studies may help diagnose performance locally, but they do not clear a new checkpoint for public weight release. Any alternative corpus must first receive source, version, attribution, license and privacy review; a paper's use of a dataset is not a grant of rights for this project.
